## Objective and reproduced defect

Close Technical Finding D without changing FFH-022 policy. Before production editing, the named regression `three spouse candidates cannot bypass explicit target-year A/B shared-family authority` failed with `17500 !== 8750`.

Exact inputs: target year 2026; A=`self`, B/C=`spouse_partner`; all active/nondependent; A/B under 55, eligible with twelve family-coverage months and zero YTD; C owns no HSA; explicit 2026 authority confirms A/B.

Exact path: three candidate people caused `candidatePairIds = []`; target-year authority and the two-candidate materiality guard were skipped; A/B fell through to independent family limits of `$8,750 + $8,750 = $17,500`. The frozen audit proves this state is persistence/input reachable because storage, capture, UI, and snapshot normalization do not enforce a maximum of two relationship candidates.

## Correction

The evaluator now retains candidate identities when cardinality exceeds two and includes them in the normalized fact map. For each HSA owner, it compares the owner month facts against every other candidate using the accepted FFH-025 materiality predicate. If both could be eligible and family coverage is known or possible, the owner receives targeted `more_information_needed` for ambiguous pair authority.

No pair is inferred from relationship, filing status, allocation rows, account ownership, order, prior-year data, or the mere existence of one authority row. Explicit A/B authority does not suppress another unresolved material candidate. Fully known all-self-only candidates remain actionable. IRA/workplace routes remain independent.
