## Objective
Resolve the FFH-012 audit authority gap created by the persisted `spouse_partner` relationship value, which does not distinguish a legal spouse from a non-spouse partner, while FFH-D005 / FFH-007 married-family HSA sharing rules are stated for spouses.

## Policy result

FFH must require explicit affirmative legal-spouse authority for the relevant person pair and target HSA tax year before married-spouse HSA shared-family ordinary-limit/allocation rules may apply.

Minimum semantic state:
- `confirmed_legal_spouses`
- `confirmed_not_legal_spouses`
- `unknown`

Exact implementation names are not mandated.

### `spouse_partner`
`spouse_partner` alone may never authorize married-spouse HSA sharing. It may identify a candidate pair for UI confirmation but is not legal authority.

### Confirmed legal spouses
When affirmative spouse authority exists, existing FFH-D005 / FFH-007 rules may apply when the approved HSA eligibility/coverage facts otherwise trigger married-family treatment: one shared ordinary family base, equal default absent a valid alternate allocation, and owner-specific nontransferable age-55 catch-up.

### Confirmed non-spouse partners
Do not create a married shared-capacity group or equal spouse split. Evaluate each owner independently under the already-approved person/month HSA rules. Any married-allocation row is non-authoritative for that pair/year.

### Unknown
Do not assume spouse sharing or non-spouse independence where the legal result would differ. Return targeted `more_information_needed` for the affected spouse-dependent HSA capacity. Independently supported self-only/unrelated routes may continue when spouse status cannot change their result.

### Tax filing status
`tax_filing_status` is corroborating only, not authoritative. The schema explicitly describes it as a planning tax filing status, not a filed return. It may not create or erase legal-spouse HSA authority. Conflicts should surface for reconfirmation/data cleanup rather than silently override the explicit legal-spouse authority.

## Minimum product/data contract

Persistence/runtime/UI must preserve a pair-specific, tax-year-bound, tri-state legal-spouse authority (or lossless equivalent) with confirmation/provenance metadata.

Required safeguards:
- default/missing = unknown;
- no optimistic backfill from `spouse_partner`;
- no backfill from `tax_filing_status`;
- no silent prior-year carryforward;
- explicit married allocation is valid only after affirmative spouse authority for the same pair/year;
- normalized runtime contract carries the same state through Existing Cash, Secure, Build, Windfall, Your Plan, hypothetical reruns, and Recommendation Refresh;
- UI explicitly distinguishes legal spouse vs non-spouse partner vs unknown for the target HSA tax year and does not preselect from ambiguous metadata.

Exact schema/table/type/UI mechanics remain App/Data/Manager design choices.

## Required regression scenarios

Engineering remediation / re-audit must cover at least:
1. affirmative legal spouses + full-year family coverage -> one shared 2026 $8,750 ordinary base and equal $4,375/$4,375 default before other existing rules;
2. confirmed unmarried partners + same HSA facts -> no married shared group/equal split; owners evaluated independently;
3. unknown/missing legal-marriage authority + material family coverage -> targeted `more_information_needed`;
4. MFJ planning filing status + unknown spouse authority -> still unresolved;
5. `single` planning status + affirmative spouse authority -> filing status cannot silently disable spouse sharing;
6. MFJ planning status + confirmed non-spouse authority -> filing status cannot create spouse sharing;
7. explicit married-allocation row + unknown/non-spouse authority -> allocation cannot create marriage/shared room;
8. unknown-safe locality for supported self-only capacity/unrelated retirement routes;
9. mixed material case where only spouse-dependent family HSA capacity blocks;
10. 2026 spouse authority does not silently authorize 2027;
11. pair/person order invariance;
12. authority change triggers recomputation and stale shared-family recommendations do not survive refresh.

Existing FFH-012 regressions for month sensitivity, Medicare, last-month treatment, YTD/tax-year isolation, catch-up ownership, multiple HSAs, cent-safe allocation, exact reconciliation, and cross-consumer behavior remain required.
