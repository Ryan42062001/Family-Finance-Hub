## Accepted authority
Family Finance Hub must require explicit affirmative legal-spouse authority for the relevant person pair and target HSA tax year before married-spouse HSA shared-family ordinary-limit/allocation rules may apply.

Minimum semantic state:
- `confirmed_legal_spouses`
- `confirmed_not_legal_spouses`
- `unknown`

Exact implementation names are not mandated.

`spouse_partner` alone is never sufficient authority. `tax_filing_status` is corroborating only and cannot create or erase legal-spouse authority. A married-allocation row cannot create marriage authority.

### Behavior
- Confirmed legal spouses: approved married-family HSA sharing rules may apply when the other HSA eligibility/coverage facts trigger them.
- Confirmed non-spouse partners: do not form a married shared-capacity group; evaluate each owner independently under the approved person/month HSA rules.
- Unknown/missing: do not infer either marriage or non-marriage when the result is material; return targeted `more_information_needed` for spouse-dependent HSA capacity while unrelated independently supported routes may continue.

## Required product/data contract
Persistence/runtime/UI must preserve a pair-specific, target-tax-year-bound tri-state legal-spouse authority or lossless equivalent, with confirmation/provenance metadata. Missing defaults to unknown. No optimistic backfill from `spouse_partner`, filing status, or a prior tax year.

The normalized runtime must carry the same authority through Existing Cash, Secure, Build, Windfall, Your Plan, hypothetical reruns, and Recommendation Refresh. UI must explicitly distinguish legal spouse / non-spouse partner / unknown for the target HSA tax year without preselection from ambiguous metadata.

## Required regression coverage
Implementation/re-audit must cover affirmative spouses, confirmed non-spouses, unknown, filing-status conflicts, married-allocation-without-authority, unknown-safe locality, tax-year isolation, pair-order invariance, and recommendation refresh after authority changes, in addition to existing FFH-012 HSA regressions.
